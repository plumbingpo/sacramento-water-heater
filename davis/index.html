How Davis Homeowners Can Extend Water Heater Life

Many premature tank failures start with maintenance that never happened, and the habits that prevent them are easy. Flush the tank once a year to clear sediment, have the anode rod inspected every few years, and operate the relief valve annually so it doesn't seize. Set the thermostat close to 120 degrees for safer water and less scale. For a tankless heater in a hard-water area, plan on descaling once a year to protect the heat exchanger.

The age of your heater matters just as much. With regular service, a standard tank usually runs 8 to 12 years, a heat pump water heater 10 to 15, and a tankless unit up to 20. Once a heater passes that range and starts showing warning signs, like discolored hot water, popping noises, or moisture around the base, it's smart to start planning a replacement. A planned replacement costs less and causes far less stress than an emergency swap with water spreading across the floor.

If warning signs do show up, acting quickly keeps them small. A dripping relief valve might point to high water pressure or thermal expansion with nowhere to go, both inexpensive fixes. The same drip ignored for months can keep stressing the tank until a seam gives way, and a small repair becomes a full replacement plus water damage. It's smart to keep our number saved on your phone so you can reach us at the first sign of trouble. Early intervention saves time, money, and cold showers.

Hot Water Headaches Davis Homeowners Face

Every type of water heater fails in its own way, and we work on all of them in Davis. Gas tanks tend to have trouble with thermocouples and flame sensors, sooty burners, and venting that doesn't draft. Tankless units show error codes for scale in the heat exchanger, ignition faults, and flow sensor problems, and many of those trace back to skipped descaling. Hybrid heat pump units depend on a clean filter and a free-flowing condensate line, and they struggle in cramped closets.
