Here is what shortens most water heaters' lives: corrosion from the inside and mineral buildup on the bottom, both of which are largely preventable. As water heats, dissolved minerals come out of the water and settle into a layer of sediment on the tank floor. On a gas unit, that layer insulates the water from the burner, so the steel runs hotter and the tank starts to rumble and pop. On an electric unit, it builds up around the lower element until the element burns out. Meanwhile the anode rod, a metal rod designed to corrode so the tank does not, dissolves a little every month. Once it is gone, the tank itself becomes the target. In Sacramento, CA, we see this pattern constantly, and most of it was preventable.

We work through a full checklist every time. The tank is drained and flushed to clear the sediment layer. The anode rod comes out for inspection and is swapped when little of it remains. The T&P relief valve is tested so we know it will open under pressure and close again cleanly. Gas heaters get a look at the burner, pilot assembly, thermocouple or flame sensor, flue, and draft. Electric heaters get element and thermostat testing and a check of the wiring connections. We also measure the air charge in the expansion tank, check the water pressure, set the thermostat around 120 degrees unless a mixing valve allows higher storage, and inspect the drain valve, connections, and pan.

Tankless water heaters have their own maintenance needs. Scale builds up inside the heat exchanger, especially in hard water, and eventually triggers flow problems, fluctuating temperatures, and error codes. We descale the heat exchanger with a pump and descaling solution through the service valves, clean the inlet filter, check the venting and condensate line on condensing models, and review the unit's error history. Warning Signs to Watch For

Water heaters usually give plenty of warning before they fail, as long as you know what to look and listen for. If any of the following sound familiar, it's worth having the unit checked:

  • Scale warnings or intermittent shutdowns on a tankless system
  • A rotten-egg smell from the hot water
  • Less hot water available than when the tank was new
  • Rusty or discolored hot water at the tap
  • Rumbling, popping, or crackling from the tank as it heats
  • Energy bills creeping up with no change in hot water use
  • Water in the drain pan or on the floor near the heater
  • More than a year since anyone flushed the tank

Small Symptoms, Big Consequences

Left alone, even minor symptoms like rumbling or temperatures that swing from hot to warm tend to mean the heater is working its way toward a breakdown. At the first hint of a problem, reach out to Sacramento Water Heater at (279) 242-2815 and we'll take a look.

What a Trained Technician Brings to the Job

Some upkeep is fair game for homeowners: watching for leaks, keeping the area around the heater clear, and noting new noises. Beyond that, the work gets riskier. Draining a tank means handling scalding water and a drain valve that may not close again if it is old and full of sediment. Pulling an anode rod often takes a breaker bar and real leverage on a fitting that has not moved in years. Testing a T&P valve on an older heater can leave it dripping afterward. On gas units, the burner, thermocouple, and venting involve combustion safety, and a vent that is not drafting can put carbon monoxide into the house.

We come ready for the common surprises: a drain valve that will not reseal, an anode rod that has turned to wire, a relief valve that drips after testing. We carry replacements, along with meters for element and thermostat testing and a descaling kit for tankless units. More than parts, we bring a trained eye. Small signs, like rust at a connection, a weak flame, or a waterlogged expansion tank, tell us where a heater is headed, and catching them early is the entire point of the visit. That is how maintenance turns into extra years of service.

Tips to Keep Your Water Heater Healthy

Routine maintenance is almost always cheaper and easier than an early replacement. Here are our recommendations:

  • Ask about water treatment if flushing turns up heavy mineral buildup
  • Stay on top of tankless descaling to avoid error codes and reduced flow
  • Flush annually, more often where the water is hard
  • Set the water temperature around 120 degrees for safety and efficiency
  • Do not store boxes, paint, or chemicals against the water heater
  • Check the drain pan and fittings every few months for drips or rust
  • Have the anode rod checked every few years and replaced when it is mostly gone
